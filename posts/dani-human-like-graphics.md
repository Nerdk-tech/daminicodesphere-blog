You can spot most AI graphics from across the room. The lighting is too even, the colors are too loud, the composition is too centered, and every surface looks like it was polished for a render farm rather than designed for a human being to look at. Impressive for a second, forgettable in a minute.

The next leap is not more detail. It is judgement. That is what we are training DANI toward: graphics that look like a person with taste made them.

## What "human-like" actually means

It is not about adding fake imperfection. It is about intent. A good designer makes decisions, and those decisions are visible in the restraint:

- **A palette of two or three colors, not twelve.** The discipline is in what was left out.
- **One focal point.** The eye is led somewhere specific instead of being given a wall of equally important things.
- **Type that has hierarchy.** A headline, a supporting line, a small detail. Different weights, different sizes, deliberate spacing.
- **Breathing room.** Empty space is a design element, not wasted canvas.
- **Symmetry broken on purpose.** Something slightly off-center because it reads better that way.

None of that requires a human hand. It requires a model that understands why a designer would choose it.

Look at what makes that graphic work. It is a crypto wallet promo, a category where most designs shout with neon gradients and floating coins. Every decision there is a human judgement call. Nothing is random, nothing is extra, and it still looks expensive. That is the standard, and that is exactly what we are teaching DANI to produce.

## Color is a language, not a mood board

Most image models treat color as decoration. We treat it as communication.

DANI is being trained to reason about color the way a designer does: contrast ratios that keep text readable, a dominant color with one supporting tone and one accent, and what specific hues mean in specific markets. Deep green does not say the same thing as electric blue, and a finance product should not look like a gaming brand.

The goal is a model that asks "what should this feel like" before it decides "what should this look like".

## Type and layout are where AI usually embarrasses itself

Ask a generic image model for a poster and it will invent beautiful nonsense text, then stack the elements in a way no designer would ever accept. We are training DANI on the boring discipline instead: readable type at real sizes, spacing that follows a grid, headings that sit where the eye expects them, and layouts that survive being viewed on a phone.

Real brand graphics are not just pretty pictures. They are functional communication, and function has rules.

## Training taste is the hard part

Anyone can generate a colorful image. The last ten percent is the difference between something that fills space and something a business would actually pay for and publish.

That means teaching DANI to choose the correct boring option over the flashy wrong one, to stop refining when the piece is already good, to keep a brand consistent across ten different graphics, and to produce work that does not need a designer to rescue it before it ships.

The Wpay graphic above is the kind of output we are aiming at as a baseline, not a showcase: clean, deliberate, brand-ready, and completely free of the AI tells.
